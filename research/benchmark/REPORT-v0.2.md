## Exploratory analysis (after the fact; does not change the verdicts)

- **Where the cost comes from.** Prompt caching worked in both runs (492 of 500 Claude Canon runs read the
  reference from cache). The cached reference fell from about 4,100 to 3,100 tokens, which at cache-read prices
  saves almost nothing. About 86% of a Claude Canon run's cost is output (thinking plus code): 451 output tokens on
  average, against 237 for TypeScript. Trimming the reference was the wrong lever; output length is the right one.
- **Detected disadvantage.** For GPT-6.2 Sol, structural distinct is higher in Canon than in TypeScript, with an
  interval that excludes 1 (1.07 to 1.77). Every other comparison overlaps 1 and is reported as no difference detected.
- **First-pass validity** rose for Claude (96.2% in v0, 97.8% in v0.2) and fell for GPT-6.2 Sol (93.0% to 90.0%);
  every miss was again fixed in a single round.
